Tteokbokki

  • Type: Snacks
  • Time: 25 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Soak the rice cakes in cold water for 10 minutes if they are hard.
  2. Bring the water or stock to a boil. Stir in gochujang, gochugaru, sugar and soy sauce.
  3. Add the rice cakes and fish cake. Simmer, stirring often, until the sauce is thick.
  4. Add the green onion, cook 1 more minute, and serve hot.

Tip: Add a boiled egg at the end for a classic street-food style meal.

Kimchi fried rice

  • Type: Rice
  • Time: 15 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Heat a little oil in a wide pan. Fry the kimchi until soft.
  2. Add the gochujang and kimchi juice and stir for 30 seconds.
  3. Add the rice, break up any clumps, and fry on high heat.
  4. Finish with sesame oil. Fry the eggs separately and place one on top of each serving.

Tip: Cold, day-old rice fries better than fresh rice because it is drier.

Gyeran-jjim (steamed egg)

  • Type: Sides
  • Time: 15 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Beat the eggs, then mix in the water, salt and saeujeot. Pour through a sieve for a smooth texture.
  2. Pour into a small heat-safe pot. Cook on low heat, stirring, until it starts to set at the edges.
  3. Cover with a lid and cook on the lowest heat until it puffs up.
  4. Top with green onion and a few drops of sesame oil. Serve in the pot.

Tip: Use equal parts egg and liquid for a firm texture, or 1:1.5 for a softer one.

Kimchi jjigae

  • Type: Soups & stews
  • Time: 30 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Stir-fry the pork and kimchi in a pot until the pork is no longer pink.
  2. Add gochugaru, gochujang, garlic and water. Bring to a boil.
  3. Reduce the heat and simmer.
  4. Add the tofu and green onion and cook 3 more minutes. Serve with rice.

Tip: The more sour the kimchi, the better the stew. Old kimchi is ideal.

Bibimbap

  • Type: Rice
  • Time: 40 min
  • Level: Medium
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Blanch the spinach and bean sprouts separately. Squeeze dry and season each with a little sesame oil and salt.
  2. Stir-fry the carrot and zucchini separately until just tender.
  3. Cook the beef with the soy sauce until browned.
  4. Fry the eggs sunny-side up.
  5. Place rice in bowls, arrange the toppings and egg on top, and add gochujang. Mix before eating.

Tip: Mix everything thoroughly at the table so the sauce coats every grain.

Japchae

  • Type: Sides
  • Time: 35 min
  • Level: Medium
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Boil the noodles, then rinse in cold water and cut into shorter lengths.
  2. Blanch the spinach. Stir-fry the carrot, onion and mushrooms separately.
  3. Toss the noodles with soy sauce, sugar, garlic and sesame oil in a hot pan for 2 minutes.
  4. Mix in the vegetables, sprinkle with sesame seeds, and serve warm or at room temperature.

Tip: Cook each vegetable separately so every one keeps its own color and texture.

Pajeon

  • Type: Snacks
  • Time: 20 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, water, egg and salt into a thin batter.
  2. Heat the oil in a pan. Lay the green onions flat in the pan and pour the batter over them.
  3. Fry until golden, then flip and fry the other side.
  4. Cut into squares and serve with the soy sauce and vinegar mixed together.

Tip: Cold water and a hot, well-oiled pan are the secrets to crispy edges.

Miyeokguk (seaweed soup)

  • Type: Soups & stews
  • Time: 40 min
  • Level: Easy
2 servings

Ingredients

Method

  1. Soak the seaweed in cold water, then drain and cut into short pieces.
  2. Heat the sesame oil in a pot and stir-fry the beef and garlic.
  3. Add the seaweed and soy sauce and stir for 2 minutes.
  4. Pour in the water, bring to a boil, then simmer.
  5. Season with salt to taste and serve with rice.

Tip: Soak the seaweed in cold water until it expands to about ten times its size.
